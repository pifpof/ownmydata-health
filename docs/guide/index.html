The idea

Why this project exists

ownmydata was created on one belief: we have the right to own our health data, and that right matters more than ever in a future where AI is becoming part of how our healthcare works.

Today your health story is scattered. A lab report in one portal, a specialist's letter in an email, a prescription in a drawer, a family history that lives only in your memory. Each clinic, insurer and system holds a piece, and none of them hold the whole picture. When AI starts helping with care, it will help most where the picture is complete and accurate. The question is who holds that picture.

  1. AI will play a bigger role in our healthcare. Of everything ahead, that is the one thing we can be sure of.
  2. AI works best with more data, and with data that is relevant and accurate. The more complete the picture of your health, the better it can help.
  3. That picture shouldn't be owned by governments, insurance companies or health providers.
  4. It's yours. You have the right to own it, and to use it however you choose.

So ownmydata gives you a way to start: a vault of plain files on your own computer, written in the standard health systems use (HL7 FHIR R4) and organised so any AI can find its way around it (the Open Knowledge Format). Nothing about it is locked in. If this project vanished tomorrow, your records would still be yours, and still readable.

The people

A community, not a product

ownmydata is open source (MIT licensed) and built by people who want the same thing: to gather our own health data, keep it safe, and use it to get better care. Better questions at the next appointment. A complete history when you change doctors. The right numbers in front of the right person. A picture of your health that an AI you choose can actually help with.

We help each other do that. Some of us write code. Most of us don't. Everyone who tries it and says what was confusing makes it better for the next person.

Use it

Ask questions, prepare for visits, catch what's still open, and bring a complete, accurate picture to the people and tools you trust.

Ways to take part, with or without code:

  • Try it and tell us what broke. Open an issue on GitHub with what worked and what didn't.
  • Share what helped. A tip about setting it up, a way you prepare for appointments, a question you wish you'd asked.
  • Make test documents. Fictional lab reports and letters in formats we haven't seen yet help the plugin read more kinds of paperwork.
  • Translate, review, build. The skills, these guides, the format, importers for other record types: all open.
One rule above all: no real health data in the community spaces. Issues, examples, screenshots and test files use fictional records only. Your real records belong in your vault and nowhere else.

The tools

How the pieces fit

There are three parts, and only one of them holds your data.

Your folder

The vault

A folder on your computer: your records as markdown files, your original documents, and a map that tells any AI where to look. It belongs to you.

Writes

The plugin

Skills for Claude that file documents, take your health history, brief you on what's open and keep the vault tidy. You talk; Claude files.

 Reads

The viewer

A Mac app for browsing your vault: sections, records, links between them. It only reads, and never goes online.

What to expect

What we promise

Where your data lives

  • Your records stay in your folder. The project's code and examples never contain anyone's real data.
  • The AI provider sees what you give it. When you hand Claude a document, its contents are processed so it can be read, as with anything you share with Claude.
  • The plugin writes only to your vault, never to Claude's account memory, never into a web search.
  • The viewer never goes online and never changes a file.

How the records stay honest

  • Copied, never computed. Values, units, ranges and flags are copied exactly as the document prints them.
  • No invented codes. A medical code is written only when the document prints it, or it matches a short, checked list.
  • Absent stays absent. If the document doesn't say it, the record doesn't either.
  • Every answer names its source.
  • No forms. You are never asked to fill in a field so a record is "complete".
Not medical advice. ownmydata keeps your records straight and tells you what they say. It doesn't diagnose, prescribe, or tell you to start, stop or change treatment. For sudden or severe symptoms it tells you to get urgent care, and so do we: if something feels wrong right now, contact a doctor or emergency services first.
